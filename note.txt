

The best design is a **dynamic Gaussian-sum branch bank with branch-local particle filters and an explicit one-step Bayesian experimental-design controller**.

That is more specific than “moving branches + birth + reinit.” It means:

[
p_t(\theta);\approx;\sum_{\ell=1}^{L_t} q_{\ell,t},p_{\ell,t}(\theta),
\qquad
\theta=(g,\phi_{\mathrm{off}},A),
]

where each component (p_{\ell,t}) is a **local PF branch** over the full parameter vector, and the branch bank is controlled by a **moving Gaussian envelope primarily along (g)**. This is exactly the direction suggested by the paper’s supplement: for multimodal posteriors, a single PF is structurally weak, and a bank of filters with different priors is the natural remedy; fixed mixture locations give only limited resolution, while allowing the component locations and scales to move turns the update into a nonlinear mixture-tracking problem. ([ar5iv][1])

For your physical model, that hybrid is better than either extreme:

* **better than one global PF** because your likelihood is oscillatory in (g), so the posterior can have multiple separated gravity aliases;
* **better than a pure Gaussian mixture** because within each branch the model is still nonlinear and coupled through (\phi_{\text{off}}), (A), and visibility/noise effects;
* **better than your current fixed-slot bank** because your current bank is still a bank of **pre-seeded (g)-regions**, not a bank of **evolving hypotheses**. Your code literally defines a “fixed-slot multi-PF bank,” seeds slots by a fixed (g)-spread parameter, and still scores performance by the global mixture mean, which is exactly the failure mode the supplement warns about.

## Why this is the right design for *your* physical model

Your gravimeter is stateless/projective at the measurement-loop level: the next outcome depends on the current control and latent parameters, not on a hidden probe trajectory. The model is Bernoulli with

[
p(y=1\mid \theta,x)
===================

\frac12\left[1+V(T,B),A\cos!\big((1+\varepsilon)k_g(T,B)g+\phi_{\text{off}}+\psi\big)\right],
]

with controls (x=(T_s,B'*p,\psi)) and latent parameters (\theta=(g,\phi*{\text{off}},A)). In your code, that is exactly the gravimeter likelihood and simulator structure.  

That matters because it tells us the posterior geometry is not arbitrary:

* the multimodality is driven **primarily by wrapped phase in (g)** through (k_g(T,B),g);
* (\phi_{\text{off}}) is a **circular nuisance parameter** coupled to (g) through the same phase;
* (A) is mainly an **amplitude/visibility nuisance parameter**;
* resource cost is explicit through the cycle time, so controls should be chosen by **information gained per resource**, not per step.  

So the correct architecture should be **multimodal mainly along (g)**, but still keep **full local PFs over ((g,\phi_{\text{off}},A))** inside each branch.

That is why the best design is **not**:

* a fixed grid over (g),
* not a full unconstrained PF bank in raw 3D without structure,
* and not a pure Gaussian-mixture-only filter.

It should be a **branch bank over (g)-modes with local PF branches over the full latent state**.

# Detailed architecture

## 1) Posterior representation

Represent the posterior as a mixture of branch posteriors:

[
p_t(\theta)
\approx
\sum_{\ell=1}^{L_t} q_{\ell,t},p_{\ell,t}(\theta),
\qquad
\sum_{\ell=1}^{L_t} q_{\ell,t}=1.
]

Here:

* (q_{\ell,t}) is the posterior probability that branch (\ell) is the correct mode;
* (p_{\ell,t}(\theta)) is the local posterior within that mode.

Each branch is represented by particles:

[
p_{\ell,t}(\theta)\approx \sum_{n=1}^{N_\ell} w_{\ell,n,t},\delta(\theta-\theta_{\ell,n,t}).
]

This is exactly the “multiple PFs in parallel whose union represents the full posterior” idea from the supplement, but with moving local representations rather than fixed ones. ([ar5iv][1])

### Intuition

A branch is not “the left bin” or “the center bin.”
A branch means:

> “One coherent explanation of the data so far.”

That is the semantic unit you need.

---

## 2) Branch parameterization: Gaussian envelope on (g), PF inside branch

For each branch (\ell), maintain:

* a branch mass (q_\ell),
* a local PF over full ((g,\phi_{\text{off}},A)),
* branch moments
  [
  \mu^g_\ell,;\sigma^g_\ell,;\mu^\phi_\ell,;\mu^A_\ell,
  ]
* and an **envelope** over (g), typically
  [
  \mathcal N(g;\mu^g_\ell,(\sigma^g_\ell)^2).
  ]

The Gaussian is **not** the full posterior model. It is the **branch envelope** used for:

* birth,
* merge,
* overlap tests,
* controller summaries,
* and compact branch bookkeeping.

The PF inside the branch carries the real nonlinear local posterior.

### Why this hybrid is best

The supplement’s “sum of Gaussians” idea is right at the mixture level, but for your gravimeter the branch-internal geometry is not well captured by a Gaussian alone, because the likelihood still depends nonlinearly on
[
k_g(T,B)g+\phi_{\text{off}}.
]
So the best architecture is a **Gaussian-sum *bank*** with **particle branches**, not a Gaussian-sum-only filter. ([ar5iv][1])

---

## 3) Branch update

For a chosen control (x_t) and observed outcome (y_t), branch (\ell) is updated internally by the usual Bayes rule:

[
\tilde w_{\ell,n,t+1}
=====================

w_{\ell,n,t},p(y_t\mid x_t,\theta_{\ell,n,t}),
\qquad
w_{\ell,n,t+1}
==============

\frac{\tilde w_{\ell,n,t+1}}{\sum_n \tilde w_{\ell,n,t+1}}.
]

The branch evidence is

[
Z_{\ell,t}(x_t,y_t)
===================

\sum_n w_{\ell,n,t},p(y_t\mid x_t,\theta_{\ell,n,t}).
]

The branch probability update is then

[
q_{\ell,t+1}
============

\frac{q_{\ell,t},Z_{\ell,t}(x_t,y_t)}
{\sum_j q_{j,t},Z_{j,t}(x_t,y_t)}.
]

This separates:

* **within-branch inference**: particle update inside a hypothesis
* **between-branch inference**: evidence competition between hypotheses

That separation is the key thing your current architecture still muddles.

---

## 4) Selective resampling inside branches

The paper notes that resampling is delicate and that resampling too early distorts the posterior; it also notes that selective resampling would be an improvement over “resample everyone together.”  

For the quality-first design, resampling should be:

* **branch-local**
* and **ESS-triggered per branch**

So branch (\ell) is resampled only when

[
\mathrm{ESS}_\ell
=================

\frac{1}{\sum_n w_{\ell,n}^2}
]
drops below a threshold.

This is much better than any mechanism that keeps branches synchronized as a batch artifact.

### Intuition

Each branch is its own local posterior object.
It should collapse, diffuse, and resample on its own schedule.

---

## 5) Birth: residual-based mode discovery

This is one of the most important steps.

Let the bank-implied marginal over (g) be

[
\hat p_t(g)
===========

\sum_{\ell=1}^{L_t} q_{\ell,t},\hat p_{\ell,t}(g),
]

where (\hat p_{\ell,t}(g)) is either:

* a KDE from branch particles,
* or the branch Gaussian envelope.

Now define the **currently explained mixture envelope**

[
\hat p^{\text{env}}_t(g)
========================

\sum_{\ell=1}^{L_t} q_{\ell,t},
\mathcal N(g;\mu^g_{\ell,t},(\sigma^g_{\ell,t})^2).
]

The residual unexplained mass is

[
r_t(g)=\big[\hat p_t(g)-\hat p^{\text{env}}*t(g)\big]*+.
]

If (r_t(g)) has a local maximum with enough mass and enough separation from existing branches, spawn a new branch centered at

[
g_\star = \arg\max_g r_t(g).
]

### How to initialize the new branch

Initialize a new local PF using:

* (g) concentrated around (g_\star),
* (\phi_{\text{off}}) and (A) either:

  * inherited from nearby posterior particles,
  * or sampled from a conditional local proposal.

This is the crucial difference from your current design.
An inactive slot is not reactivated at a fixed preset location.
It is reinitialized **where the unexplained posterior mass actually is**.

### Intuition

Birth should answer:

> “What plausible explanation is missing from the bank?”

not:

> “Can I split one pre-existing slot?”

---

## 6) Merge

Branches (\ell) and (m) should merge when they represent essentially the same hypothesis.

A good merge test is based on:

* overlap in (g)-envelopes,
* circular phase closeness,
* (A)-closeness,
* and ideally similarity in predicted outcome probabilities under relevant controls.

For example, merge if:

[
D_{\text{Bhat}}(\mathcal N_\ell,\mathcal N_m) < \tau_g,
]
[
d_{\mathbb S^1}(\mu^\phi_\ell,\mu^\phi_m) < \tau_\phi,
]
[
|\mu^A_\ell-\mu^A_m| < \tau_A.
]

Then form the merged branch by:

* combining branch masses,
* pooling particles,
* resampling a new local PF from the union.

### Intuition

Merge should mean:

> “These two branches no longer correspond to distinguishable explanations.”

---

## 7) Prune

If a branch remains low-mass for long enough, prune it:

[
q_{\ell,t}<\tau_{\text{prune}}
\quad\text{for }P\text{ consecutive steps}.
]

Use patience, not instantaneous pruning, because transient evidence dips are normal.

### Intuition

Prune says:

> “This hypothesis has effectively lost the competition.”

---

## 8) Reinitialization of inactive slots

Inactive slots are not dead memory. They are **capacity for future modes**.

When birth happens, an inactive slot is reinitialized from the residual mode.
That means inactive slots should not retain stale geometry from old branches.

This is the correct use of fixed-capacity memory.

# Controller architecture

Here is where quality-first design differs most from your current approach.

## 9) The controller should not reason over all branches symmetrically

Your current bank-aware controller still aggregates a lot of information through a softened branch average and global mixture summary. That is why it tends to learn a compromise policy instead of a mode-discriminating one. The current code explicitly feeds global summaries, top-two-gap-like signals, and per-slot branch summaries into a controller, while evaluation is still based on the global mixture mean.  

For a high-quality architecture, the controller should focus on the **top active ambiguities**:

* branch 1,
* branch 2,
* maybe branch 3 if needed.

Because a Bernoulli measurement gives only one bit per shot.
Trying to optimize equally over 5 tiny branches is usually wasteful.

---

## 10) Best controller objective: one-step Bayes design, not pure end-to-end RL

Because your physical model is known and the outcome is binary, the cleanest quality-first control architecture is:

### choose (x_t) by maximizing a one-step expected utility

[
x_t^\star = \arg\max_x U_t(x).
]

The best utility for your problem is a **mixture of refinement and discrimination**:

[
U_t(x)
======

-\lambda_{\text{risk}}
\underbrace{
\mathbb E_{y\sim p(y|x,\mathcal B_t)}
\Big[
\mathrm{tr}(G,\Sigma_{t+1}(y;x))
\Big]
}*{\text{expected posterior risk}}
+
\lambda*{\text{disc}}
\underbrace{
I(B_t;Y_{t+1}\mid x,\mathcal B_t)
}_{\text{branch discrimination}}
\Big/
c(x),
]

where:

* (\mathcal B_t) is the current branch bank,
* (B_t) is the discrete branch index,
* (Y_{t+1}\in{0,1}),
* (G) is your nuisance-weighting matrix,
* (c(x)) is the resource cost (cycle time).

Because the measurement is Bernoulli, the branch-information term has a very simple form.

Let

[
\pi_\ell(x)=\mathbb E_{p_{\ell,t}(\theta)}[p(Y=1\mid \theta,x)],
]
[
\bar\pi(x)=\sum_\ell q_\ell \pi_\ell(x).
]

Then

[
I(B;Y\mid x)
============

## h(\bar\pi(x))

\sum_\ell q_\ell,h(\pi_\ell(x)),
]

where (h(p)=-p\log p-(1-p)\log(1-p)) is Bernoulli entropy.

### Why this is so important

This term explicitly rewards controls that make competing branches predict different outcomes.

That is exactly what your current learned controller is *not* directly trained to do.

---

## 11) Resource-normalized design is essential

Your gravimeter resource count is cycle-time-based, not step-based. In the code, used resources are incremented by the cycle time derived from (T_s). 

So the controller should maximize **information per resource**, not raw information per shot.

That means a long (T_s) is only good if the increased sensitivity is worth the extra time.

This matters a lot because (k_g(T,B)) scales nonlinearly with (T_s) and (B'_p), while cost also grows with (T_s).

---

## 12) Microwave phase should be optimized for branch discrimination or branch refinement

Because your likelihood is
[
\frac12[1+V A\cos(k_g g+\phi_{\text{off}}+\psi)],
]
the phase control (\psi) is special.

For local refinement of a dominant branch, you want near-quadrature operation.

For discriminating two top branches (\ell_1,\ell_2), you want (\psi) such that

[
|\pi_{\ell_1}(x)-\pi_{\ell_2}(x)|
]
is large.

So in a quality-first architecture, I would not let the NN “freely discover” phase from scratch. I would compute (\psi) by either:

* direct 1D optimization inside the candidate-control search,
* or an analytic/quadrature-style rule conditioned on the top ambiguity.

This is a model-based advantage you should exploit.

# Best initialization for branches

## 13) Branch centers should be physics-informed, not uniformly spaced

A very important physical insight:

The multimodality in (g) is induced by phase wrapping in
[
k_g(T,B),g+\phi_{\text{off}}+\psi.
]

So the characteristic alias spacing in (g) is approximately

[
\Delta g \approx \frac{2\pi}{k_g(T,B)}.
]

That means branch placement should be tied to plausible phase-wrap spacing under informative controls, not an arbitrary uniform grid over the prior.

### Better initialization

Choose a reference informative control (x_{\text{ref}}), compute (k_{\text{ref}}=k_g(x_{\text{ref}})), and seed branch centers roughly one alias spacing apart:

[
\mu^g_\ell \approx g_0 + m_\ell \frac{2\pi}{k_{\text{ref}}}.
]

This is physically meaningful.
It is much better than “5 evenly spaced slots in the prior.”

# Final recommendation

If quality is the goal, then the best architecture is:

## Dynamic Gaussian-sum branch bank + branch-local PFs + exact one-step Bayes control

### Posterior layer

[
p_t(\theta)\approx \sum_\ell q_{\ell,t} p_{\ell,t}(\theta)
]
with moving branch envelopes in (g) and local PFs over full ((g,\phi_{\text{off}},A)).

### Lifecycle layer

* birth from residual unexplained (g)-mass
* merge by overlap/similarity
* prune by low evidence with patience
* inactive-slot reinit at actual residual modes

### Control layer

[
x_t^\star = \arg\max_x
\frac{
-\lambda_{\text{risk}}\mathbb E_y[\mathrm{tr}(G\Sigma_{t+1}(y;x))]
+\lambda_{\text{disc}} I(B;Y|x)
}{
c(x)
}
]

### Physics exploitation

* branch initialization based on alias spacing (2\pi/k_g)
* phase chosen to maximize local slope or top-branch discrimination
* resource normalization by cycle time

That is the most intuitive, technically sound, and physically matched design for your case.
